Decimals & percentages

Finding percentages of amounts

Years 5–6 6 min read Practise this →

Per cent means “per hundred”. 35% is 35 out of every 100, or the fraction 35/100.

You almost never need to calculate that directly. Instead, build the percentage you want out of a few easy ones — 50%, 25%, 10%, 5% and 1% — and add or subtract. It is faster than any formula and it works without a calculator.

The method, step by step

  1. 1

    Learn the five building blocks

    50% = halve. 25% = halve twice. 10% = divide by 10. 5% = half of 10%. 1% = divide by 100. Every other percentage is a combination.

  2. 2

    Break the percentage into blocks

    35% = 25% + 10%. Or 30% + 5%. Either works — pick whichever gives easier numbers.

  3. 3

    Work out each block from the original amount

    Always from the original, never from a running total.

  4. 4

    Add them together

    That is the answer.

  5. 5

    For an increase or decrease, add or subtract from the whole

    A 20% discount on £80: 10% = 8, so 20% = 16, and you pay 80 − 16 = £64. Or go straight to 80% of 80 = 64.

Worked examples

Where it usually goes wrong

✕ Calculating the discount and giving it as the price.

✓ 20% off £80 is a £16 discount and a £64 price. Re-read what the question asked for.

✕ Taking each percentage off the running total.

✓ All the blocks come from the original amount. 35% is 30% of the original plus 5% of the original.

✕ Assuming a 20% rise then a 20% fall returns you to the start.

✓ It does not. £100 → £120 → £96, because the fall is 20% of the larger number.

✕ Dividing by the percentage.

✓ To find 25% of 80 you divide by 4, not by 25. Convert the percentage to a fraction first if that helps.

The fraction shortcut

Some percentages are much faster as fractions:

50% = 1/2 · 25% = 1/4 · 75% = 3/4 · 20% = 1/5 · 40% = 2/5 · 10% = 1/10 · 12.5% = 1/8 · 33⅓% = 1/3

“75% of 48” is three quarters of 48: divide by 4 to get 12, multiply by 3 to get 36. Far quicker than building from 10%.

Reverse percentage problems

“After a 20% discount a coat costs £36. What was the original price?”

£36 is 80% of the original. So 1% is 36 ÷ 80 = 0.45, and 100% is 45. The original price was £45.

The trap is taking 20% of £36 and adding it back, which gives £43.20 — wrong, because the 20% was of the larger original. Always find 1% of the known percentage first.

Percentage change

To find a percentage change, work out the difference, divide by the original, and multiply by 100.

A price rises from £40 to £50. The difference is £10; 10 ÷ 40 = 0.25; that is a 25% increase. Note it is not 20% — dividing by the new price is the standard error.

Questions people ask

What is the quickest way to find 10%?
Divide by 10 — shift every digit one place right. 10% of 46 is 4.6.
How do I find a percentage without a calculator?
Build it from 50%, 25%, 10%, 5% and 1%. Almost every percentage in a primary question is a sum of two or three of those.
Is 'percentage of' the same as 'fraction of'?
Yes. A percentage is a fraction with denominator 100, so the method is identical.
Why isn't a 20% rise cancelled by a 20% fall?
Because the second percentage is taken from a different, larger amount. £100 rises to £120, and 20% of £120 is £24, bringing it to £96.
